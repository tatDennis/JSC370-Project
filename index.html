Income vs. Inflation: Understanding Housing Affordability in Canada Across Generations

Dennis Tat

2025-04-25

Introduction

With the next federal election just around the corner, issues of affordability and housing access have taken center stage in public discourse. Canada has experienced a notable increase in the cost of living over the past decade, evident by the rising inflation. These economic pressures have outpaced the growth of salaries, making it increasingly difficult for many Canadian young adults entering the workforce to achieve financial stability. For this demographic, home ownership has become an increasingly distant prospect. At the same time, those who already own property have seen their real estate assets appreciate significantly in value, contributing to a growing sense of inequality between generations.

This widening gap has fueled a prevailing narrative that older generations had a far easier path to financial security and home ownership than today’s youth. While anecdotal evidence and media commentary have popularized this belief, the goal of this study is to perform empirical analysis to determine the extent to which this perception is grounded in economic reality.

This study seeks to explore these dynamics by examining the relationship be- tween inflation, housing prices, and salaries, in order to provide a clearer picture of how economic conditions have evolved over time.

Research Questions

To investigate the aforementioned imbalance in spending power between younger and older generations, this study will evaluate a multitude of economic factors. Several key questions will guide the analysis:

Has the median income of Canadians kept pace with inflation over time?

How has median income evolved across age groups, especially for younger versus older Canadians?

How does the change in income compare to the rise in housing prices?

Finally, can housing prices be predicted by analyzing the price trends of other consumer goods?
